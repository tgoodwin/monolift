## Critique of codex's trace

### Path
- **Correct:** partially — The steps are technically correct in terms of execution order, but the trace is far from minimal.
- **Minimal:** no — At 23 steps, this is an exhaustive system trace, not a minimal activation path. It includes the entire "provisioning" sequence (Steps 7–14) which describes how the objects are created and wired together; this belongs in resolution notes, not the activation path itself. Steps 3–6 are also redundant direct calls that could be collapsed.

| 16 | `http-server-handler-callback` | OK | `interface-method-dispatch` | While framework-specific, it correctly identifies the library-to-app transition. |

### Other notes
Codex's trace is more of a "how Caddy works" document than a "minimal static path." While technically impressive in its detail, it violates the minimality constraint by including the full provisioning lifecycle.

## Cross-trace observations

The traces converge on the high-level flow: `CLI -> net/http -> ServeHTTP -> Middleware -> Authenticate`. They meaningfully diverge on the inclusion of the provisioning phase; Codex treats it as part of the activation path, whereas Claude and Gemini treat it as setup/resolution context. Claude's trace is the most professional and closest to a canonical minimal path, as it uses language-level edge types and focuses on the runtime execution flow.
