### Summary

- **Path length:** 4 steps (entrypoint → region root).
- **Edge types encountered:** `goroutine-launch-of-concrete-method` (×2), `method-call-on-concrete-type` (×2).
- **Hardest edge:** None of the four edges are hard. The two goroutine launches (Steps 1–2) are the only edges that add any complexity at all, because a compiler must understand that the callee runs on a separate goroutine and any instrumentation/dispatch around it must be goroutine-safe — but the callee identity itself is statically pinned.
- **Static-analysis feasibility:** Fully resolvable. The entire path uses concrete receiver types and direct method calls; a standard call-graph + type-flow analysis (no points-to required, no interface resolution required, no reflection involved) connects `main()` to `(*Campaign).CompileTemplate` without ambiguity.
- **Alternative paths (not traced):** `CompileTemplate` is also reachable from six HTTP-handler call sites — `cmd/campaigns.go:176` (`PreviewCampaign`), `cmd/campaigns.go:646` (`sendTestMessage`), `cmd/campaigns.go:715` (`validateCampaignFields`, used by `CreateCampaign`/`UpdateCampaign`), `cmd/public.go:178`, `cmd/archive.go:247`, and `cmd/templates.go:245`. These paths are longer and harder to resolve statically because Echo registers handlers as `echo.HandlerFunc` values (e.g. `g.GET("/api/campaigns/:id/preview", pm(hasID(a.PreviewCampaign), ...))` at `cmd/handlers.go:165`), threading the handler method through higher-order wrappers (`pm`, `hasID`) and storing the resulting closure in the router's data structures — an `http-handler-registration` plus `closure-capture` chain that requires whole-program function-value tracking to resolve.
