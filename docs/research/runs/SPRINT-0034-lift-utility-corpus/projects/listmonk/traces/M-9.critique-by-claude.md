## Critique of codex's trace

### Path
- **Correct:** yes — every file:line citation verifies. `initHTTPServer` is at `cmd/init.go:888`, `initHTTPHandlers` at `cmd/handlers.go:33`, `pm = a.auth.Perm` at `cmd/handlers.go:84`, the `g.POST("/api/tx", ...)` at `:196`, `(*Auth).Perm` header at `internal/auth/auth.go:336` with the closure body opening at `:337` (codex points at the body, which is fine for "to" semantics), the `next(c)` happy-path return at `:365`, `SendTxMessage` at `cmd/tx.go:17`, `m.Render` call at `cmd/tx.go:132`, region root at `models/messages.go:74`.
- **Minimal:** debatable — the trace splits handler-registration from wrapper-invokes-next into two edges (Steps 3 and 4) rather than collapsing both into one "framework-dispatched-callback" edge. Splitting is more faithful to what a static analyzer must do (track the bound-method value into a closure that later calls it as `next(c)`); collapsing is shorter. Both are defensible. No step is genuinely fabricated or padded.

### Other notes

## Cross-trace observations

All three traces converge on the same canonical path: `main → initHTTPServer → initHTTPHandlers → POST /api/tx registration → SendTxMessage → m.Render`. The only meaningful divergence is path granularity at the wrapper edge: codex splits it into registration + closure invocation (5 steps); my trace and gemini's collapse it (4 steps). Codex's split is the most faithful to what a static analyzer actually does, but my collapsed version with explicit `closure-capture` framing in the resolution notes captures the same information at one fewer step. Gemini's trace, while reaching the same path, under-describes the closure-capture mechanism in its resolution notes and has one minor file:line drift (`init.go:894` vs `:888`); it is the weakest of the three on edge-type analysis but not on path correctness.
