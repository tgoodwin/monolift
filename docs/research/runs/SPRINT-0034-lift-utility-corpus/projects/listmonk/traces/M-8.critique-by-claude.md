## Critique of codex's trace

### Path
- **Correct:** yes — every file:line citation checks out (`main` at `cmd/main.go:193`, `initBounceManager` at `cmd/init.go:792`, `bounce.New` at `internal/bounce/bounce.go:67`, the `m.mailbox = mailbox.NewPOP(...)` assignment at `:79`, `Run` at `:118`, `runMailboxScanner` at `:135`, the dispatch site at `:138`, `(*POP).Scan` at `internal/bounce/mailbox/pop.go:79`).
- **Minimal:** no — step 3 ("Store the POP implementation in the mailbox interface field") is a data-flow edge masquerading as a control-flow step. The actual call-graph path from `main` to `(*POP).Scan` does not pass through that assignment; the assignment is a *side effect* of step 2's call into `bounce.New` and is the fact that lets the dispatch in step 6 resolve. Either fold it into the resolution note for step 6 (gemini's choice) or attach it as a side-effect on step 2 (my choice). As a numbered path step it inflates the path. Steps 1 and 2 are both legitimate control-flow edges and should not be collapsed; `initBounceManager` is not a trivial forwarder.

## Cross-trace observations

All three traces converge on the same call-graph spine: `main` → (construction) → `go Run` → `go runMailboxScanner` → `m.mailbox.Scan` ⇒ `(*POP).Scan`, and all three identify the interface dispatch as the only non-trivial resolution. They diverge on **whether the type-binding evidence is a path step**: codex makes it step 3, I fold it as a side-effect on the construction step, gemini demotes it to a resolution note. Of the three, gemini's is closest to a canonical *minimal control-flow* path (3 steps) but loses fidelity on construction details and has two off-by-a-few-lines citations on the entrypoint side; codex is the most explicit but mis-categorizes a data-flow edge as a path step and uses non-uniform edge-type granularity. The right schema, I think, is gemini's path shape with codex's explicit `interface-implementation-binding` annotation attached as a separate edge class (data-flow, not on the numbered path).
