## Critique of codex's trace

### Path
- **Correct:** partially — Step 2 attributes `routers.InitWebInstalled(...)` at `cmd/web.go:205` to `runWeb`; that call is in `serveInstalled` (`cmd/web.go:166`). `runWeb` reaches `serveInstalled` via `return serveInstalled(cmd)` at `cmd/web.go:287`. Steps 6–7 splice in a *producer-side* HTTP path (NormalRoutes → `HookPostReceive`) that is not on the static-reachability spine from `main` to `pushUpdates`; it explains how items get *enqueued*, not how the queue worker reaches the region root. The activation chain runs through the goroutine/channel plumbing already wired in Step 5.
- **Minimal:** no — Step 1 collapses several edges (cli framework dispatch, `Action: runWeb` field write, `app.Run` callback) into one CLI macro edge, while Step 9 collapses goroutine launch + `RunWithCancel` + interface dispatch on `RunCanceler` + `batchChan` send/receive + `doStartNewWorker` worker goroutine + `doWorkerHandle` into one "queue-work-item-dispatch" macro edge. These two macro edges hide most of the static-analysis difficulty. Steps 6–7 should be cut entirely from the static path; the HTTP enqueue path is at most a parenthetical alternative trigger.

## Critique of gemini's trace

### Path
- **Correct:** partially — Steps 1–8 are correct (with minor redundancy noted below). Step 9 ("From: push.go:48 `CreateSimpleQueue(... handler)` → To: push.go:36 `handler`") draws a direct edge from registration to invocation. That edge does not exist statically: the call chain that actually invokes `handler` runs through `RunWithCancel` → `WorkerPoolQueue.Run` → `doRun` → `doStartNewWorker` (worker goroutine) → `q.batchChan` receive → `doWorkerHandle` → `q.safeHandler` (closure) → `q.origHandler(t...)` → `handler`. By collapsing Step 9 into a single arrow, the trace asserts as a static fact something that requires a goroutine launch, a typed channel value-flow, and a closure-captured struct field read to make true. Step 10 then reads as "handler calls pushUpdates" with no source for the call to `handler` itself.
- **Minimal:** no — but the problem is the opposite of codex's. The trace is too short *because it skips activation mechanism*, not because steps were collapsed for compactness. Steps 1–4 are also inflated: Step 1 (`main → NewMainApp`), Step 2 (`NewMainApp → newWebCommand`), Step 3 (`newWebCommand` writes `Action: runWeb`), and Step 4 (`app.Run` invokes `runWeb`) span four edges to express one resolution problem (function value in `cli.Command.Action` field is invoked by the cli library). Steps 1–2 are pure construction edges that don't change reachability; collapse them into the Step 3/4 pair.

### Other notes
- The trace never mentions goroutines, channels, generics, `RunCanceler`, or the `safeHandler`/`origHandler` field pair. For a queue-backed activation path these are the load-bearing mechanisms — eliding them undersells the static-analysis difficulty and means the path as drawn is *not* a complete reachability witness.
- Step 5 uses `cmd/web.go:287` to reach `serveInstalled` at `:166` — this is the step claude's trace omits and codex's misattributes. Gemini got that piece right.
- Step 1's "main → NewMainApp" edge (`main.go:44 → cmd/main.go:88`) is correct but pure construction; `NewMainApp` returns the app value, it does not transfer control toward the region root. Construction edges only matter when they *register* a function value reachable later.

## Cross-trace observations

All three traces converge on the same backbone: `main` → cli framework → `runWeb` → `InitWebInstalled` → `mustInitCtx(repo_service.Init)` → `initPushQueue` → `CreateSimpleQueue("push_update", handler)` → `handler` → `pushUpdates`. They diverge sharply on how they treat the queue plumbing between `CreateSimpleQueue` and `handler`: my trace expands it into ~5 mechanism-level edges (goroutine launch ×2, interface dispatch on `RunCanceler`, channel send/receive on `batchChan`, closure-captured field invoke on `safeHandler`), codex collapses it into one "queue-work-item-dispatch" macro edge while also splicing in the producer-side HTTP path, and gemini elides it entirely by treating registration as if it were activation. They also disagree on edge-label granularity: codex tends to framework-level names (`cli-command-action-dispatch`, `http-handler-registration`, `queue-work-item-dispatch`), gemini uses observational names (`callback-execution`), and my trace stays at the language-mechanism level (`function-value-in-struct-field`, `interface-method-dispatch`, `channel-send-receive`). For the canonical minimal path the language-mechanism granularity is the right grain — framework names hide the resolution problems a compiler must solve, and observational names don't tell the analyzer what to do; my trace is closest to that grain, though Steps 1 and 5 in codex (compressed cli setup, generic constructor chain) point at edges I should consider expanding further (`generic-type-parameter-instantiation` in particular).
