- **Minimal:** yes — Every step represents a necessary resolution boundary for a compiler. The inclusion of Step 7 (HTTP dispatch) is particularly insightful as it highlights the interface-mediated gap between registration and execution.

### Other notes

## Critique of codex's trace

### Path
- **Correct:** yes — The path follows the same valid UI-based route as Claude.
- **Minimal:** yes — The steps are necessary, though Step 4 is slightly pedantic in its labeling.

### Edge types
| Step | codex's label | Verdict | Suggested label (if different) | Reasoning |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2/3 | `conditional-direct-function-call` | RENAME | `direct-function-call` | Conditionality is a property of the control flow (branching), not the edge type itself. The resolution mechanism is a simple direct call. |
| 4 | `struct-literal-field-assignment` | RENAME | `direct-function-call` | The primary activation edge is the call to `newRouter()`. The assignment of the result is secondary. |
| 5 | `http-handler-registration` | RENAME | `direct-function-call` | In `ui.Serve(...)`, the function is invoked directly to *obtain* the handler. The registration happens to the *result* of this call. Claude's separation is more accurate. |

### Other notes
Codex's use of "conditional" prefixes adds noise without changing the required resolution logic. It also merges the registration and dispatch of the UI handler, which obscures the most difficult static analysis boundary.

## Cross-trace observations

The traces converge on the UI handler path (`internal/ui/entry_save.go`), which is one of several valid paths (API, Google Reader, and Fever also reach `SendEntry`). Claude's trace is the most rigorous and closest to a canonical path because it explicitly identifies the `net/http` interface dispatch as a distinct resolution step. All traces correctly identify the `goroutine-launch` as the final edge to the region root.
