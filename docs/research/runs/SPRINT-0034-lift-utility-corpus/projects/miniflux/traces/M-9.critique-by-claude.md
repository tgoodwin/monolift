# Trace critique for miniflux/M-9 by claude

## Critique of codex's trace

### Path
- **Correct:** yes — every file:line citation verifies against the source. Path is real and complete.
- **Minimal:** mostly. Step 4 ("struct-literal-field-assignment") describes value-flow, not a separate call edge — the actual call `newRouter(store, pool)` is the same direct call that gemini and I label. Splitting the value-flow out as its own labeled step is a re-labeling of Step 3→4, not an extra hop. Conversely, codex collapses the runtime dispatch edge (`(*ServeMux).ServeHTTP` → `saveEntry` body) into Step 6→7, jumping straight from the registration site at `ui.go:104` to the goroutine launch at `entry_save.go:36`. The hardest edge on this path is precisely that dispatch boundary; eliding it understates the analysis cost.

### Other notes
- The "From → To" framing for Step 6 is slightly misleading: at `ui.go:104` the method-value `handler.saveEntry` is *stored* in the mux's route table, not invoked. Codex's narrative collapses the storage and the eventual invocation into one edge, which loses the dispatch step.
- Inconsistency: Step 4 picks a value-flow label while Step 5 reuses a registration label for what is also a direct call. The trace would be cleaner if it picked one labeling axis (call-edge vs. value-flow) and stuck to it, or used composite labels everywhere.
- The alternative-paths note (REST/GoogleReader/Fever) is useful and accurate.

## Critique of gemini's trace

### Path
- **Correct:** yes — citations verify. Note that gemini's "Step 1" begins at `cli.go:248` (Parse → startDaemon), folding the `main` → `cli.Parse` edge into the entrypoint preamble rather than numbering it. That's a presentational choice but the path is complete.
- **Minimal:** yes. 6 numbered steps; gemini drops the value-flow distinction codex makes at server.go:49 and treats it as a plain direct-function-call, which I think is correct. The path is the closest of the three to the canonical minimal call-edge sequence.

A more granular schema would split these into a registration edge (storage) and a dispatch edge (interface-method-call). Gemini collapses them. |
| 6 | `goroutine-launch` | OK | — | Correct. |

### Other notes
- Gemini's resolution notes are the thinnest of the three traces — Step 5 just says "static analysis must connect the method value to its definition," which is true but doesn't articulate that the connection requires modeling the `net/http.ServeMux` storage→`ServeHTTP` retrieval pattern. For a schema-building exercise, the prose is too coarse.
- No mention of alternative paths (REST/Fever/GoogleReader). Codex and I both note them; gemini doesn't.
- The `Hardest edge` summary correctly identifies `http-handler-registration` as the analysis bottleneck but its rationale ("hierarchical nature of `http.ServeMux` delegation in `newRouter`") points at delegation between two muxes; the actual hardness is the route-table-to-dispatch edge, not the mount-point delegation.

## Cross-trace observations

All three traces converge on the same call-edge sequence: `main → cli.Parse → startDaemon → StartWebServer → newRouter → ui.Serve → mux.HandleFunc(saveEntry) → go SendEntry`. The meaningful divergences are (1) whether the `(*ServeMux).ServeHTTP` dispatch step is materialized as its own edge — only my trace splits it out; codex and gemini collapse it into the registration step — and (2) edge-label vocabulary at server.go:49 (codex: value-flow; gemini & me: call-edge) and at routes.go:46 (codex: registration; gemini & me: call-edge). Gemini's path is closest to the canonical minimal call-edge sequence; codex's adds a value-flow re-labeling that breaks vocabulary consistency; my own trace is the most granular but materializes the dispatch boundary that the other two leave implicit. For a general schema, the registration/dispatch pair should probably be two labels rather than one — codex and gemini both lose information by fusing them.
