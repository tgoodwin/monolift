The activation edge is the subsequent `Start()` call; the constructor only justifies why the receiver is concrete and belongs in resolution notes, not as a path step. Collapsing both yields a 9-step path that matches the canonical shape.

## Critique of gemini's trace

### Path
- **Correct:** partially. Several drift errors:
  - Step 1 cites `main.go:21`; the actual call is on line **20** (line 21 is `os.Exit(1)`).
  - Step 4 attributes the call at `server.go:104` to `serverCmdF`, but line 104 is inside **`runServer`** — `serverCmdF` calls `runServer` at line 57, and `runServer` is what invokes `server.Start()`. The trace silently elides the `serverCmdF → runServer` edge.
  - Step 6 cites `server.go:626` for `rcs.Start()`; the actual call is at **line 625** (line 626 is the error return).
  - Step 8 cites `send.go:50` for "receives a task from `rcs.send[idx]`"; the receive is at **line 45**, and line 50 is the body of the `case sendFileTask:` arm calling `rcs.sendFile`.
- **Minimal:** no in one direction, yes in another. Step 2 introduces an extra node (`cobra.Command.Execute`) as the call target — that is a library entry, not a node on the activation path. The path edge a compiler needs is `RootCmd.Execute() → serverCmdF` (mediated by the `RunE` field). On the other hand, gemini collapses `serverCmdF → runServer → server.Start()` into a single step, which is over-collapsing — `runServer` is a real, non-trivial intermediate function.

### Edge types
| Step | gemini's label | Verdict | Suggested label (if different) | Reasoning |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2 | `method-call-on-concrete-type` | DROP | — | This step shouldn't exist as a path node. `cobra.Command.Execute` is library code; the analytically interesting edge is the next one (Execute → RunE field). |
| 3 | `callback-invocation` | RENAME | `function-value-in-struct-field` | "callback" names a framework concept (cobra callbacks). The language-level mechanism is "method/function value stored in a struct field, called through that field" — codebase-agnostic and what a compiler actually has to resolve. |
| 7 | `goroutine-launch` | OK | — | — |
| 8 | `type-switch-dispatch` | RENAME | `channel-receive-type-switch` | The label hides the harder half. The `t.(type)` switch only becomes resolvable once you know the dynamic-type set flowing through `chan any`; that requires inter-procedural value-flow over channel sends, not just type-switch handling. |

### Other notes
- The "From"/"To" framing in Step 3 is unusual: it labels the registration site (`server.go:30`) as the From and the function body (`server.go:39`) as the To, when the call actually originates from inside `cobra.Command.Execute`. Either the From should be the Execute call site (`root.go:17`) — which makes Step 2 redundant — or the step should describe the field-dispatch and cite the field rather than the registration line.
- The elision of `runServer` in Step 4 means the trace skips a real function frame. A compiler still has to walk through it; labelling the edge as direct-from-`serverCmdF` is incorrect.

## Cross-trace observations

All three traces converge on the same activation path: `main → commands.Run → RootCmd.Execute → serverCmdF → runServer → (*Server).Start → startInterClusterServices → (*Service).Start → go sendLoop → channel-receive + type-switch → sendFile → sendFileToRemote`. They diverge mostly on granularity and label vocabulary: codex inflates with constructor sidebars (`NewServer`, `NewRemoteClusterService`) for an 11-step path; gemini under-specifies by collapsing `runServer`, interposing a `cobra.Command.Execute` library node, and accumulating small line-number drift; the canonical minimal path is 9 steps and most closely matches my own (claude) trace. On edge-type vocabulary, gemini's `callback-invocation` and `type-switch-dispatch` are framework-flavored / under-specified, while codex's `init-time-function-field-dispatch` over-commits to the init phase but otherwise tracks the same mechanism I labelled `function-value-in-struct-field`.
