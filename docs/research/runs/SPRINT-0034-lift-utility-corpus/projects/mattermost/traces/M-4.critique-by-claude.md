## Critique of codex's trace

### Path
- **Correct:** yes — every file:line citation I spot-checked (`main.go:19`/`:20`, `root.go:15`/`:17`, `server.go:30`/`:36`/`:39`/`:57`/`:60`/`:73`, `app/server.go:191`/`:405`/`:513`/`:514`/`:536`/`:572`/`:1479`/`:1498`/`:1499`, `init.go:24`, `elasticsearch/indexing_job.go:25`/`:38`, `common/indexing_job.go:39`/`:137`/`:164`/`:165`/`:224`/`:284`/`:337`/`:342`/`:363`/`:390`/`:412`, `jobs/server.go:72`/`:79`/`:87`, `jobs/workers.go:37`/`:47`/`:51`/`:52`/`:56`, `jobs_watcher.go:78`/`:81`) is accurate.
- **Minimal:** no — Steps 1, 6, 7, 8, 9, 10, 11 are *resolution side-conditions* for the goroutine-launch edge (Step 14), not control-flow steps on the path from `main` to `BulkIndexPosts`. The init-time registration (Step 1) actually runs *before* `main`, so as a numbered "Step 1 in the path from main" it is chronologically misplaced. Step 6 (option apply) and Step 12 (field-guarded call) are also predicate/data-flow facts, not separate call edges. Folding Steps 1, 6, 7, 8, 9, 10, 11 into a single resolution note attached to the goroutine-launch edge would compress this to ~11–12 steps without losing fidelity. Step 13 also compresses two real call frames (`JobServer.StartWorkers` at `server.go:79` → `Workers.Start` at `workers.go:47`) into one step while citing the inner target — pick one or split. Step 15 (Watcher) is on a *parallel* goroutine that produces the channel value consumed in Step 16; it is not on the activation path to `BulkIndexPosts` and would be cleaner as a sidebar note about the channel-send producer.

### Other notes
- The trace is the most *thorough* of the three, and the line citations are reliable. The cost is edge-type labels that name framework/control-flow patterns ("field-guarded", "timer-guarded", "data-guarded", "factory") rather than the underlying static resolution mechanism. A general schema needs to separate (a) the call-resolution mechanism from (b) the predicates governing whether the call fires.
- Treating registration steps and option-flow as separate path steps will not generalize: every Go binary using cobra+option-functions+init-registered factories would balloon the path. A cleaner model: the *path* is the call sequence; *resolution* of each edge is captured in notes that may transitively reference init-time registrations and field writes.

## Cross-trace observations

All three traces agree on the same backbone: `main` → cobra dispatch → `serverCmdF` → `runServer` → `app.NewServer` → ... → `runJobs` → `Jobs.StartWorkers` → goroutine launch of `IndexerWorker.Run` → channel-receive → `DoJob` → `IndexBatch` → `IndexPostsBatch` → `BulkIndexPosts`. The meaningful divergence is *what counts as a path step*: my trace treats init-time registration, option-flow, and worker-map population as resolution side-conditions on the goroutine-launch edge (11 steps); gemini lifts the factory call and interface dispatch into discrete steps but folds option-flow into prose (14 steps); codex makes nearly every dataflow fact a numbered step (20 steps), including the parallel watcher goroutine and the worker-map write. On edge-type labels, codex over-specifies with framework/control-flow predicates ("field-guarded", "timer-guarded", "data-guarded", "factory", "by-constant-key") while gemini under-specifies ("callback-registration", "channel-receive" used as the call edge itself); my own trace sits between but should make the predicate-vs-mechanism split more explicit. Closest to the canonical minimal path: my own (11 steps with side-conditions in notes), with gemini next once its line numbers are corrected; codex is the most reliable on citations but the least minimal and most codebase-bound on labels.
