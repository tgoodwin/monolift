## Critique of codex's trace

### Path
- **Correct:** partially — The path eventually reaches the root, but it includes side-branches and redundant lifecycle steps.
- **Minimal:** no — Steps 4 (`NewServer`) and 8 (`server.Start`) are unnecessary setup operations that do not lie on the direct activation path. Steps 9 and 10 attempt to model the runtime HTTP dispatch loop, which is already captured by the `http-handler-registration` abstraction in shorter traces.

### Edge types
| Step | codex's label | Verdict | Suggested label (if different) | Reasoning |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2 | `init-time-function-field-dispatch` | OK | — | Good label that combines the timing and mechanism of the Cobra dispatch. |
| 9 | `http-route-dispatch` | RENAME | `http-handler-registration` | The trace from `main` should ideally stop at registration or use a single registration edge to jump to the handler. Modeling the internal `net/http` loop adds unnecessary complexity. |
| 11 | `conditional-direct-function-call` | RENAME | `direct-function-call` | The "conditional" nature is a property of the call-site guard, not the edge type itself. |

### Other notes
Codex's inclusion of `server.Start` (Step 8) and the subsequent `ServeHTTP` dispatch (Step 9-10) shifts the trace from a "minimal static path" to a "system lifecycle trace," which diverges from the goal of identifying the direct connection between the binary entrypoint and the region root.

## Cross-trace observations

The traces converge on the core route: `main` -> `Cobra CLI` -> `server setup` -> `APIv4 registration` -> `UploadFileX` -> `postprocessImage`. Claude and Gemini are nearly identical in their 9-step minimal path. Codex meaningfully diverges by explicitly modeling the server startup and the HTTP request-response loop, resulting in a significantly longer 13-step path that includes non-essential setup steps. Claude's trace is the most precise in its labeling of the underlying Go mechanisms (e.g., function values in struct fields).
